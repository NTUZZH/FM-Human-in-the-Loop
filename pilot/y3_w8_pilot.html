Which job should go first?

A short study on how facility supervisors rank maintenance work orders. You will see 56 pairs of real work orders. For each pair, say which of the two you would start first.

How long it takes. About 35 to 45 minutes. You can stop and come back: the page keeps your answers as long as you do not close the tab.

Anonymous, aggregate use. We do not record your name, your employer, or anything that identifies you. Answers are reported only as group statistics (how often practitioners agree with each other) in a research paper on automating work-order dispatch. There are no right answers and nothing is being tested about you. You may stop at any time and your partial answers can simply be discarded.

Where the jobs come from. Every work order shown is a real, already completed record from a public research dataset of North American university campuses (FMUCD, Mendeley Data, CC BY-NC). The text is shown as it was written.

Before you start

Please fill these in. The participant code is whatever the person who sent you this file asked you to use (for example R1); it is how your sheet is labelled, not who you are.

     

Instructions

  1. Each item shows two work orders that are sitting in the same site's queue at the same moment. Assume a team member is free now and can start either one.
  2. Pick the one you would start first, on operational urgency: what happens if it waits another day, who or what is affected, and how far it has already slipped.
  3. You may disagree with the recorded priority. It is shown because a supervisor sees it, not because it is correct.
  4. Answer from your own judgement and go with your first instinct. Around 40 seconds per item is normal.
  5. The optional one-line reason is genuinely useful to us, but skip it whenever you are not sure what to write.
  6. Some campuses record a building descriptor and some do not, so a few items carry one line fewer. The two jobs within an item always show the same fields, so neither is ever better documented than the other.

What the recorded priority means

Recorded priorityTarget response time
P1 Emergency1 business day
P2 Urgent3 business days
P3 Routine10 business days
P4 Planned21 business days

Fields you will not see, because a supervisor does not have them when the job is dispatched: how long the job actually took, what it eventually cost, and when it was closed out. "Estimated labour" is the work content a planner would estimate up front.
